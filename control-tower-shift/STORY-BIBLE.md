# Story Bible — The Oathbearer

Status: narrative canon for the linear story mode at `/#control-tower-rpg`. `RPG-VERTICAL-SLICE.md` owns the implementation contracts; `ACTS-II-V-BLUEPRINT.md` owns region objectives, encounter IDs, and acceptance gates. Where narrative wording conflicts, this document governs character motive, revelation order, restoration consequences, and ending logic.

## Originality boundary

This is an original Greek-myth-inspired story about language, consent, memory, and public promises. It does not copy characters, plot, factions, dialogue, locations, map layouts, visual identity, quest structures, branded terminology, or systems from *Hades*, *Assassin's Creed Odyssey*, or another game. It specifically excludes mercenary ladders, conquest battles, naval loops, assassination trees, procedural contracts, randomized loot tiers, repeated camps, and a gear treadmill. Greek gods and mythic creatures are shared source mythology; their characterization, dramatic function, and every mortal character here are original to this project.

## One-sentence premise

When a former archivist steals the relational titles that bind gods to places, storm-born courier **Kallias**, a son of Zeus who chose a mortal life of service, must carry those living promises through five wounded regions, discover his own part in the lie that began the theft, and build a covenant that neither gods nor mortals can control alone.

## Story pillars

1. **A name is a relationship, not a collectible.** An epithet has force only because a place, a power, and witnesses agree what it promises.
2. **Restoration is interpretation.** The main path is linear, but each repaired promise leaves a specific, revisitable regional state.
3. **Institutions can preserve peace and preserve harm at the same time.** The Keepers and gods are not secret ultimate villains; they made defensible choices with human costs.
4. **The antagonist is right about the wound and wrong about the cure.** Damas correctly identifies coerced divine bargains, then removes the language people need to refuse him.
5. **Companions carry continuity.** Regional people matter, but Kallias, Thessa, Ianthe, Amonides, and Doria sustain the emotional argument across all five acts.

## The metaphysics of epithets

An epithet is a witnessed verb attached to a divine name: Apollo **Far-Sighted** warns travelers; Poseidon **Harbor-Keeper** keeps a negotiated shoreline; Persephone **She-Who-Returns** makes departure reversible. It is neither a god's full identity nor a magic key. It is a compact among four parts:

- **Power:** the deity or older force capable of fulfilling it.
- **Place:** the terrain and community in which it operates.
- **Words:** the limits, beneficiaries, and cost of the promise.
- **Witnesses:** living or dead people who can attest that the promise was accepted.

The **Veil Accord** prevents a god from acting directly in the mortal world without an epithet anchored in all four parts. A god may advise Kallias through a patron shrine because Kallias carries the treaty-stone fragments; the god cannot simply descend and solve a region.

### What theft does

The Silent Loom does not pick up an object. It cuts one part out of the relationship and forces the remaining parts to compensate. Every theft must manifest in six authored channels:

| Channel | Production rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Map | Connections lose meaning, not merely visibility. Use authored before/after routes. | A harbor road becomes a tidal channel because “safe arrival” no longer distinguishes road from sea. |
| Memory | NPCs retain facts but lose the relationship that makes them coherent. | A sailor remembers a song and a rescue, but not that the singer was a friend. |
| Language | Affected verbs/nouns become circumlocutions, blanks, or conflicting labels. Never remove accessibility semantics. | “Return” becomes “arrive again” until Persephone's promise is witnessed. |
| Terrain | The place literalizes the broken promise through finite state variants. | Harvest and winter occupy the same orchard in alternating authored overlays. |
| Relationships | Duties become possession, gratitude becomes debt, or kinship becomes recordkeeping. | Poseidon reads protection as ownership after Harbor-Keeper is fused to Oceanus's Boundary. |
| Enemies | Creatures imitate the missing verb or attack its witnesses. | Time-wraiths repeat a route; medusae pin people into one remembered role; hydra heads echo contradictory orders. |

The effect is never random or universal amnesia. A content definition identifies the stolen epithet, affected lexicon, map variant, named NPC memory fractures, relationship changes, enemy overlays, and restoration outcomes. Main-objective labels remain readable in neutral language even when story-world labels are damaged.

### What restoration does

Kallias cannot restore an epithet by returning it to a pedestal. A valid restoration requires:

1. recover the severed words;
2. learn who benefited and who paid;
3. gather at least one willing divine witness and one mortal/local witness;
4. defeat the creature or machine feeding on the contradiction;
5. ratify wording that determines the post-act region state.

Each act offers 2–3 authored formulations. They never change act order or block completion. They change terrain access on revisit, relationship lines, one non-required convenience route, one enemy behavior overlay, and evidence available for the final Accord. Consequences are previewed in plain language before confirmation and can be reviewed at the Witness Board.

## Protagonist and antagonist

### Kallias, the Oathbearer

Kallias is canonically male and uses he/him pronouns. He is a son of Zeus and a mortal woman, Neria of Kastron Vale, but he is not a prince, prophesied savior, hidden god, or claimant to Olympus. His divine ancestry gives him an innate storm current; it does not give him political authority, moral certainty, immunity from harm, or a special right to the Veil Accord. His history, personality, pronouns, lighter brown hair, and pale husky-blue eyes are fixed. Cosmetic options may vary complexion details, scars, and clothing accents without changing who he is.

Kallias became a courier because a delivered message has a sender, destination, and receipt—clean obligations in a world of divine ambiguity. He became an Oathbearer because his life taught him that a clean chain of custody can still carry an unjust lie.

#### Life before the game

This chronology is the story's respectful mythic translation of a life shaped by public trauma, military service, medicine, study, civic work, and resistance. The attack and war are not spectacle or a shortcut to toughness. They leave grief, habits, moral injury, friendships, and a commitment to care that the story treats without glamorizing violence.

1. **The Year of the Last Laurel:** Kallias is born in Kastron Vale, a small inland town, during the final year later generations call the Golden Age. The title is retrospective: prosperity was real for some, uneven for others, and already maintained by distant wars. Neria raises him. Zeus acknowledges Kallias privately but offers neither a throne nor a stable presence.
2. **The seventh year / the Breach of Xenia:** at a Panhellenic feast near Kastron, rival envoys accept food and shelter beneath the protection of Zeus Xenios. A disputed oracle—possibly altered between sanctuary and assembly—brands them oathbreakers. Their hosts murder them after the welcome rite has made them guests. Seven-year-old Kallias does not witness a spectacular attack; he witnesses trusted adults turn a public promise into its opposite, the sacred hearth burn blue, and competing readers insist that the same words both required and condemned the killing. Some hosts claim they prevented a planned betrayal; surviving attendants say the accusation was manufactured; the original oracle disappears into an emergency archive. Retaliation becomes the **War of Redress**, and temporary emergency powers become the permanent law of Kallias's youth. The event ends his innocence by teaching him that protection can be revoked through language while its symbols remain standing.
3. **Kastron Civic Academy:** Kallias spends two years at his hometown's practical academy studying letters, civic measures, and basic healing. With few routes beyond Kastron and a sincere wish to keep people alive inside a war he did not choose, he joins the imperial service. The story does not frame enlistment as destiny or uncomplicated patriotism.
4. **Route medic:** Kallias trains as a battlefield healer and convoy courier. He crosses islands, deserts, mountain borders, and occupied provinces stabilizing wounded soldiers and civilians, carrying medicine, casualty letters, and disputed field reports. His storm current can restart a failing heartbeat, cauterize in extremis, and charge his spearhead or free hand, but it cannot reverse death or make care painless.
5. **The Orthe packet:** eight years before Act I, route-medic Kallias carries a sealed Keeper report away from storm-struck Orthe. Amonides tells him the seal will summon aid. It is actually a revised account that removes the villagers' rejected terms from the official record. Kallias delivers it faithfully and builds a life around never opening what is entrusted to him.
6. **Pillar City of Alke:** after leaving the cohorts, Kallias studies at the Crown Lyceum in a towering mortal metropolis built around the civic works attributed to Hercules. The city calls its highest terraces “little Olympus.” He studies stewardship of soil, water, food, waste, and public works: how human settlements can live within mortal limits instead of treating land and labor as inexhaustible.
7. **The Iron Laurel's first hundred decrees:** Kallias graduates as a new administration takes power under the Iron Laurel. He becomes a junior aide in the Assembly of Common Seats, expecting to translate stewardship into law. During the regime's first hundred decrees he watches emergency rules centralize authority, erase testimony, and turn relief records into loyalty tests.
8. **The Common Lanterns:** when civic channels close, Kallias leaves the Assembly and joins a decentralized resistance of medics, printers, stewards, veterans, tenants, and public witnesses. He runs care stations, documents removals, escorts threatened speakers, and carries petitions between regions. The Lanterns use disruption and civil defense; the story does not turn activism into a consequence-free combat fantasy.
9. **Where play begins:** Kallias arrives at Beacon Overlook carrying Lantern testimony to Amonides just as Far-Sighted is severed and the treaty-stone breaks. He initially tries to complete the delivery. He turns back by choice to pull Thessa and trapped townspeople from the collapsing court, speaks the names on the testimony aloud when the shrine forgets them, and accepts responsibility for what he carries. Only then do the fragments lodge in his shadow.

The treaty-stone does **not** answer Zeus's blood. Other children of gods have touched it without response, and Zeus cannot command it to choose. It answers the pattern Kallias made through healing, study, civic work, resistance, courier service, and the opening choice to become an accountable witness. His ancestry explains his storm; his decisions explain the Accord.

#### Innate storm combat

Kallias always fights with a long bronze spear as his primary weapon, a hooked xiphos holstered for emergencies, close-range storm strikes through his free hand, and a charged evasive step. This is his embodied history as route medic and son of Zeus—not a patron slot and not a second spell tree.

- **Stormhand:** canonical presentation for his existing basic/melee strikes. Electricity communicates impact and may chain only where the core combat configuration explicitly supports it.
- **Stormstep:** canonical presentation for movement/dash feedback. It grants no undocumented invulnerability or traversal bypass.
- **Medic's discipline:** explains deliberate recovery, ally interactions, and checkpoint care. It is not an instant resurrection power.
- Zeus's `thunderbolt` remains the canonical active patron power from `POWER_DEFS`. Innate storm visuals must not duplicate its chain-strike effect, cooldown, damage, or power ID.
- Kallias may choose any unlocked patron. Patron abilities represent negotiated aid layered over his baseline storm identity; his father is not auto-equipped and does not own Kallias's power.

**Want:** repair the Accord quickly enough that no other place suffers Asterion Reach's collapse.

**Need:** accept that faithful delivery is not moral neutrality; a witness must sometimes question the terms he carries.

**Fear:** that opening, interpreting, or revising a message makes him as controlling as the people who wrote it—and that public power, divine or civic, inevitably turns care into control.

**Arc:** healer under orders -> civic servant -> resistant courier -> implicated witness -> accountable author. Kallias never becomes ruler of the gods; he becomes one signatory among many.

### Damas of Orthe, the Quiet Regent

Damas was First Archivist under Amonides and father to Elia, a harbor engineer. Orthe's people rejected a new divine levy, believing the old covenant still protected them while talks continued. A storm destroyed the village. The gods blamed ambiguous wording; the Keepers revised the minutes to prevent the entire Veil Accord from collapsing; Amonides authorized the revision. Elia died after leaving testimony that the revision would erase the villagers twice—first from protection, then from history.

Damas found the revision in the packet Kallias delivered. He built the **Silent Loom** as an emergency archive that could preserve epithets outside divine control. When the Keepers refused a public reckoning, he concluded that any relational promise could be manipulated by its witnesses. He became the Quiet Regent: if no one could speak an epithet, no god could enforce one and no institution could forge one. He recruits people harmed by bargains they never consented to.

**Want:** end divine leverage and Keeper revision by removing epithets from use.

**Need:** recognize that silence is also rule, and that storing relationships without the people in them preserves syntax while killing meaning.

**Fear:** another official account will make Elia's refusal disappear.

**Fatal choice:** he lets the Loom consume ordinary relational words—friend, return, shelter, witness—to stabilize its archive. At that moment his refuge becomes a regime.

**Final possibility:** Damas can acknowledge Elia's actual testimony before defeat, but he does not evade accountability or become the final author. The ending is about what replaces his method, not whether the player excuses him.

## Recurring companion cast

### Thessa — field keeper and first companion

Pragmatic, impatient with ceremony, and good at reading ruined shrines. Thessa begins believing a repaired institution needs better people, not different rules. She was a junior copyist when Orthe's record arrived and remembers Amonides ordering a second clean copy. She did not know what was removed and has spent years refusing to ask.

- **Gameplay/narrative role:** explains epithet manifestations, logs witness statements, anchors checkpoint conversations.
- **Arc:** loyal technician -> horrified participant -> advocate for public, reviewable records.
- **Relationship with Kallias:** mutual professional respect strained by the discovery that both “just did their jobs.” No romance dependency.
- **Act presence:** field companion in I–III, witness-board coordinator in IV, co-author in V.

### Ianthe — adversary, defector, and skeptical companion

Ianthe is Damas's adult student and chief field operative, not his child or unthinking lieutenant. Her family was displaced by a harvest oath that privileged temple stores over tenant farms. She joins the Unnamed because Damas publishes harms the Keepers buried. She deliberately leaves Kallias alive in Act I: the treaty fragments require a mobile witness, and she hopes Kallias will carry proof of institutional failure back to Amonides.

- **Gameplay/narrative role:** decodes Unnamed machines, challenges convenient restorations, provides a non-divine reading of clues.
- **Arc:** disciplined saboteur -> witness to the Loom's civilian erasure -> conditional ally -> public accuser and builder.
- **Reversal:** in Act III she learns Damas has fed Elia's own testimony into the Loom, replacing Elia's words with an idealized refusal. Ianthe defects because his cure has committed the crime that formed him.
- **Act presence:** observed antagonist in I, direct rival in II, temporary ally/defector in III, companion in IV–V.

### Amonides — Keeper elder and compromised mentor

Amonides is the institutional memory of the Veil Accord. He revised Orthe's record because three gods threatened to withdraw from hundreds of active covenants if the contradiction became public. His decision did preserve peace elsewhere; it also denied Orthe's dead their stated refusal.

- **Gameplay/narrative role:** remote counsel at shrines, source of historical context, eventual witness against his own office.
- **Arc:** controlled disclosure -> defensive justification -> full testimony -> surrender of unilateral Keeper authority.
- **Boundary:** he is not revealed as the mastermind and is not cheaply forgiven. His useful act is making the archive auditable and accepting judgment.

### Doria — cartographer of witness paths

Doria is an Asterion stonemason whose younger brother forgets their relationship when Far-Sighted is stolen. She records routes through textures, knots, and silhouettes as well as words, so her maps partially survive language damage.

- **Gameplay/narrative role:** maintains the revisitable world map and fast-travel Witness Paths; reports concrete post-restoration changes.
- **Arc:** treats maps as neutral surfaces -> learns every route privileges someone -> designs a network whose destinations must consent to being connected.
- **Act presence:** optional introduction in I becomes mandatory before Act II departure; travels with the group through V.

### Zeus and Hercules — father, elder half-brother, and competing inheritances

Zeus recognizes Kallias as his son but mistakes acknowledgment for relationship. He admires Kallias's civic career and storm current as proof that Zeus's order can operate through mortal institutions. Kallias remembers instead how often distant authority asked healers and families to absorb its costs. Their conflict is not “reject or accept being divine”; it is whether inherited power grants authorship over other people's lives.

Hercules is Kallias's much older half-brother. The Pillar City of Alke has turned Hercules's labors into civic monuments, recruitment murals, and a story that strength naturally deserves command. The living Hercules is more ambivalent: he knows service can become propaganda once someone else writes the inscription. He initially needles Kallias for hiding a thunderer's strength inside medicine and paperwork; Kallias challenges him for letting monuments simplify whom his labors hurt and helped.

Forge March gives the brothers a shared task rather than an instant reconciliation. Hercules holds a failing gate while Kallias treats trapped smiths and routes the load away from Atlas. Hercules learns that endurance without stewardship merely transfers weight; Kallias accepts that visible force can protect a public process without owning it. Hercules then stands beside, not above, the mortal witnesses. His optional `herosWrath` patron power remains canonical and is never a bloodline upgrade for Kallias.

### Companion staging budget

Only one field companion is physically present in traversal at a time; others appear at the regional hub, shrines, or fixed conversation scenes. Companions do not require combat AI. They move between authored anchor points, become non-targetable during encounters, and never block paths. Each act budgets:

- one four-character hub scene;
- two paired traversal conversations;
- one conflict scene before the boss;
- one post-restoration scene;
- optional side-story inserts only for participating companions.

## Four original factions

### 1. Keepers of the Accord

Archivists, shrine technicians, couriers, and witnesses who maintain the Veil Accord. Their strength is continuity across generations; their danger is treating a stable record as more important than a living refusal. Thessa and Amonides expose its internal split between public revision and protected authority.

### 2. The Unnamed

A coalition of displaced tenants, failed shrine beneficiaries, former archivists, and name-cutters led by Damas. They document genuine abuse and sabotage epithet anchors. Their masks carry no rank system and are not collectible targets. After Ianthe defects, members split between deactivating the Loom, preserving Damas's archive, and pursuing total silence.

### 3. Concord of Many Altars

The negotiating body through which Olympian, chthonic, and older powers maintain the Veil. Zeus, Hera, Hades, Demeter, Persephone, Poseidon, Oceanus, Nyx, and the rest keep distinct motives. It is a forum, not a unified “god faction.” Its strength is reciprocal limitation; its danger is equating divine participation with divine consent from mortals.

### 4. Free Witnesses

Regional councils formed by sailors and nereids in Pelagos, farmers and shades in Kore, and smiths and displaced workers in Forge March. They are not a single army. They share a protocol: no covenant is valid without a public local account and a way to withdraw. Their testimony makes a mortal-authored final Accord possible.

## Revelation ladder and five-act emotional spine

### Act I — The Broken Beacon / “Ash at Dawn”

**Surface problem:** Apollo's Far-Sighted epithet is severed; Acropolis Entry Court and Sun Court fill with beasts that attack warning bells and witnesses.

**Character movement:** Kallias arrives as a Lantern courier wanting to deliver testimony and the fragment to any authority who can fix the crisis. His decision to turn back for people rather than complete the packet awakens the treaty-stone. Thessa teaches the four-part epithet model. Doria's brother remembers every shared task but calls her “the mason,” demonstrating relational loss.

**Revelation:** Ianthe used Keeper cipher and spared Kallias intentionally. The optional `sq-lost-witness` finds an Orthe receipt bearing Kallias's route-medic mark under a newer Keeper seal.

**Restoration question:** Is sight a god's warning from above, a road community's shared duty, or a jointly audited beacon?

**Boss/content anchor:** Name-Cutter elite in Sun Court; existing `enc-act1-entry` and `enc-act1-sun`.

**Turn:** The fragment carries a route to Pelagos, proving the thefts form a sentence rather than random attacks.

### Act II — The Salt Covenant

**Surface problem:** Poseidon's Harbor-Keeper and Oceanus's World-Boundary are fused. Tides erase the difference between arrival and invasion.

**Character movement:** Ianthe argues the harbor was never asked whether it wanted divine protection. Kallias sees the Unnamed evacuate civilians before sabotaging a shrine. Thessa finds her own youthful handwriting in Damas's copied archive.

**Revelation:** The Archive Leviathan protects—not destroys—stolen clauses. Damas originally built the Loom as a backup outside divine custody. The optional “Unmoored Heart” proves an epithet can persist in mutual memory without an official name.

**Restoration question:** Should safety prioritize harbor livelihood, the sea's boundary, or a negotiated crossing with shared cost?

**Turn:** One cipher folio is the Orthe revision. Kallias recognizes the packet route but not yet its contents.

### Act III — The Withered Year

**Surface problem:** the Fields of Kore cannot distinguish departure from abandonment or return from repetition.

**Character movement:** Persephone rejects restoration that makes her return compulsory; Demeter fears any gap becomes another permanent loss. Kallias enters Hades's lawful gate to hear testimony the living archive omitted.

**Midpoint reversal:** Kleio names Kallias as the courier who carried the falsified Orthe report. Amonides admits he authorized it. Damas's grievance is substantiated; Kallias's moral neutrality collapses.

**Antagonist reversal:** Ianthe discovers Damas replaced Elia's specific testimony (“no covenant without our refusal recorded”) with his own absolute doctrine (“no covenant”). She defects, but Thessa does not trust her.

**Optional mystery:** “The Cup Between Seasons” reveals the Keepers used a ceremonial renewal to date the revised minutes before the villagers' refusal, making the forgery appear lawful.

**Restoration question:** Should the seasonal promise favor dependable continuity, protected departure, or a witnessed cycle with explicit release?

**Boss/content anchor:** Winter Mother Echo, a memory constructed from Demeter's fear and the Loom's false version of Elia.

**Turn:** The false constellation over Forge March is not a weapon alone; it is the Loom's replacement witness—a sky that always agrees with its author.

### Act IV — The False Constellation

**Surface problem:** Prometheus's stolen fire presses epithets into bronze stars while Atlas is forced to hold the counterfeit sky.

**Character movement:** Athena and Ares debate method. Hercules, Kallias's older half-brother, joins him in freeing Atlas and the mortal smiths; their argument about heroic force versus patient stewardship lets both demonstrate that power can serve without becoming authorship. Zeus addresses both sons, offering to stabilize every covenant under a single divine crown and frame the brothers as proof of his benevolent order. Kallias refuses his father's inheritance as a claim to rule, now understanding that benevolent unilateral control repeats both Amonides and Damas.

**Revelation:** Amonides and Damas designed the first emergency archive together after an earlier divine dispute. Amonides stopped it when it began flattening contradictory testimony; Damas finished it after Orthe. The optional Atlas tablets show the machine omits stars that require more than one observer, proving its design cannot encode disagreement.

**Restoration question:** Should craft be licensed by a careful authority, governed by the workers who bear its cost, or shared through revocable local hearths?

**Boss/content anchor:** Name-Press Colossus. Breaking each die restores one contested word to companion dialogue before the core is vulnerable.

**Turn:** A mortal draft is possible, but the Silent Loom activates and begins consuming ordinary relational language to survive.

### Act V — The Last Name

**Surface problem:** Night Stair, the false sky, and the Silent Loom erase relationships faster than the party can record them.

**Character movement:** Thessa publishes the Keeper archive. Amonides testifies without bargaining for absolution. Ianthe insists Damas hear Elia's unedited statement. Doria's nonverbal maps hold the route when place-names vanish. Kallias stops treating the draft as a packet to deliver and openly argues its terms.

**Final reversal:** Elia did not ask for the gods to vanish. She asked that a refusal remain part of every future agreement. Damas has preserved her name while erasing the condition that gave it meaning.

**Optional proof:** “A Light No Map Remembers” establishes that independent observers can sustain a shared truth without one owning the light. It earns the strongest plural-witness amendment.

**Boss/content anchors:** Loom Guardian, then the Quiet Regent. Ianthe or a neutral Keeper provides the interruption witness, so optional content never gates completion.

**Resolution:** Kallias restores the stolen epithets in act order, defeats the Loom's coercive defense, and chooses an Accord form supported by the evidence the party assembled. Damas lives or dies only through authored boss resolution, not a binary execution prompt; the constitutional choice remains the climax.

## Revisitable open regions and fast travel

The world is a chain of five bounded, revisitable regions, not a seamless continent. Each region opens linearly, then remains available after its act except during the final Silent Loom lock.

### Witness Path rules

1. Doria activates a region's first **Witness Stone** after Kallias arrives on foot through the story transition.
2. Fast travel is available only between activated Witness Stones whose local community has accepted connection. A shrine is a patron-switch/checkpoint location; it is not automatically a travel node.
3. Travel is disabled during combat, dialogue, a boss pocket, unresolved transport, or a temporary terrain transition. The UI states the reason.
4. Travel never enters a dungeon beyond its last cleared threshold or bypasses a main-objective gate.
5. Current patron, quest state, restoration formulation, terrain variant, cleared encounters, companion hub positions, and optional-story state persist on revisit.
6. Completed non-boss encounters stay cleared. Authored “aftershock” creature groups may appear only when their stable IDs and one-time flags are defined; there are no random respawns or radiant contracts.
7. Before entering `silent-loom-approach`, the game gives a clear point-of-no-return warning. Long-range travel is then disabled until the final save. `nyx-foothold` remains a safe return node within Act V.
8. After the epilogue, all accepted Witness Paths reopen. Bosses remain defeated; region consequences and unresolved optional stories remain playable.

### Revisit purpose

- see terrain, vocabulary, routines, and routes produced by the chosen restoration;
- hear one post-act companion exchange and regional reaction set;
- finish the region's optional story;
- use newly accepted Witness Paths or non-required convenience routes;
- review evidence and restoration wording at the Witness Board.

No revisit is required to unlock the next main act.

## Earned ending framework

The final choice is constitutional, not a mercy/kill prompt and not a good/evil meter. Kallias presents an Accord form, then uses earned evidence to add protections. Main-story evidence guarantees at least one complete ending; optional evidence improves specificity and public trust rather than determining victory.

### Base forms

| Ending ID | Availability | Promise | Cost/risk | Strong epilogue when earned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bounded-patrons` | authority >= 3 | Gods act only inside narrow published limits with Keeper audits. | Clear crisis response, but institutions may become rigid or capture the record again. | With `evidence-orthe-receipt` and withdrawal evidence, archives become public and affected communities can trigger review. |
| `mortal-witness` | autonomy >= 3 | Each region authors and may withdraw its own divine covenants. | Strong consent, but protection and standards vary between regions. | With mutual-memory and backdated-rite evidence, Witness Paths share refusals and aid without central rule. |
| `renewed-compact` | reciprocity + plurality >= 5; fallback if neither other threshold is met | God, Keeper, and local witness must all sign; contradictions remain in the record. | Broad legitimacy, but negotiation is slower and deadlock is visible. | With plural-stars and independent-light evidence, no signatory can erase a dissenting account. |

If no threshold is met due migrated or partial data, `renewed-compact` appears in a limited emergency form and the epilogue states its unresolved weaknesses. The player never reaches a choice screen with no valid option.

### Damas, Amonides, and Ianthe in every ending

- Damas's unedited archive and Elia's statement become public. His fate follows the authored final confrontation; constitutional form does not absolve him.
- Amonides resigns Keeper authority and submits his testimony to the chosen review mechanism. He may help preserve records but cannot lead the replacement.
- Ianthe signs as an accuser, not as representative of all harmed people. If her trust flags are low, she still testifies but declines a formal role.
- Kallias refuses permanent regency and returns to the Witness Paths as a courier authorized to question terms.

## Dialogue and tone rules

- Gods speak from domain and history, not modern quip archetypes. They may be witty, but never interchangeable.
- Mortals disagree about material costs: food, passage, burial, work, shelter, and who is heard.
- Exposition is attached to a present decision, changed place, or damaged relationship. No codex paragraph is required to understand a main objective.
- Humor comes from craft, travel, personality, and divine scale colliding with practical work—not mockery of emotional stakes.
- No faction is reduced to disposable masked enemies. Named Unnamed members can surrender or evacuate; fixed combat targets are constructs, corrupted creatures, or operatives who choose violence in the scene.
- A restoration preview states: who gains authority, who can revise it, what route changes, and what known risk remains.

## Production guardrails

- Five acts, five accepted open regions, one linear main quest chain, and one mystery-bearing optional story per region.
- Four core mortal companions; only one traversal companion active at a time; no companion combat AI or approval meter required.
- Three restoration formulations per act where budget allows; never fewer than two. Each uses authored state variants, not systemic world simulation.
- Consequences reuse existing map pockets, NPC anchor points, enemy overlays, and dialogue conditions. No formulation creates a wholly separate level.
- One post-restoration reaction set per region (6–10 lines across named NPCs), one convenience-route variant, and one enemy-overlay difference are sufficient to prove consequence.
- Main-story bosses and IDs remain: Name-Cutter elite, Archive Leviathan, Winter Mother Echo, Name-Press Colossus, Loom Guardian, and Quiet Regent.
- Existing arena power IDs and combat behavior remain unchanged; patron identity changes flavor and optional contextual affordances, never required solutions.
- Kallias is always male in authored text and performance. His baseline strikes/dash carry storm presentation, while Zeus's `thunderbolt` and Hercules's `herosWrath` remain distinct patron powers with unchanged IDs and effects.
- No seamless world, randomized faction war, naval loop, conquest layer, procedural bounty system, romance dependency, loot rarity tiers, gear treadmill, or copied quest template.

## Narrative acceptance criteria

1. A first-time player can state Kallias's want, Damas's grievance, the Keepers' compromise, and why the Loom is harmful without reading the codex.
2. Every act demonstrates at least four of the six epithet-theft channels and resolves them through the selected authored restoration state.
3. Kallias's Orthe implication is foreshadowed in I, made probable in II, confirmed in III, acted upon in IV, and answered in V.
4. Ianthe's defection follows the Elia evidence; it is not caused by losing a fight or immediate friendship with Kallias.
5. Thessa and Amonides each acknowledge a specific action they took. Neither institutional guilt nor repair is assigned only to abstract lore.
6. Every optional regional story yields a documented evidence ID, an understandable mystery insight, and a valid neutral fallback if skipped.
7. Revisiting a completed region shows the chosen terrain/route, memory/language, relationship, and enemy-behavior consequences without reopening the main boss.
8. Fast travel cannot bypass story gates, strand the player during Act V, or reset a restoration/quest flag.
9. Every eligible ending states a meaningful benefit, cost, and earned safeguard. No ending is labeled canon, good, bad, paragon, or renegade.
10. Full narrative content remains within the originality boundary above and passes an IP review before final art, naming, and marketing lock.
11. The opening makes clear that Kallias is Zeus's son and has innate storm combat, then demonstrates through action and dialogue that the treaty-stone responds to his chosen record of care and witness—not heredity, prophecy, or divine command.
12. Kallias's soldier, healer, student, civic-aide, activist, and courier history each informs at least one present-tense decision; none exists only as a heroic résumé or a substitute for processing harm.
