1. Introduction & scope

Nutrition Tracker is a personal-use web app for logging food and tracking daily nutrition. You log food by scanning barcodes, photographing nutrition labels, searching, or entering it by hand, and the app shows your running macro and micronutrient totals against targets you set.

This policy covers the app and its supporting server (the “service”) operated under tryrerun.com. The operator is [LEGAL ENTITY / OWNER NAME]. It does not cover the independent third-party services the app connects to, each of which has its own privacy policy (see Third parties & sub-processors).

2. Information we collect

The app is built around a single core of data you create by using it:

  • Food log entries — the foods you log, how many servings, the time logged, and an optional meal label (breakfast/lunch/dinner/snack).
  • Foods — the product and nutrition records the app saves and caches: name, brand, barcode (when present), serving size, calories, and macro/micronutrient values (protein, carbs, fat, fiber, sugar, sodium), plus the raw lookup response the app keeps for re-parsing and debugging.
  • Daily targets — the nutrition goals you set, kept as a versioned history so you can change them over time.
  • Wearable connection data — if, and only if, you connect a wearable (see section 6): the OAuth access and refresh tokens issued to the app, a display label for the connected account (for example its email), and the daily activity/energy metrics the app reads to show your energy balance.

Depending on how the app is deployed, this data is stored either in a PostgreSQL database (for example a hosted Neon database) or in a local file on the server. The app has no user accounts and no login: it is designed for personal, single-operator use, so it does not collect names, passwords, or profile information to identify you.

Images of nutrition labels you photograph are sent for one-time processing (see wearables and third parties); the app keeps the structured nutrition values that come back, not the photo itself.

3. What we do not do

  • No advertising. The app contains no ads and no advertising or analytics trackers.
  • No selling of data. We do not sell, rent, or trade your data to anyone, and we do not share it for anyone else’s marketing.
  • No profiling for third parties. Your food log and health metrics are used only to show you your own numbers inside the app.

4. How information is used

Information is used only to make the app work for you:

  • To look up nutrition data for a barcode, a photographed label, or a search term.
  • To record your food log and calculate daily totals against your targets.
  • To display an “energy balance” (calories in versus calories out) when you have connected a wearable.
  • To cache products you have already looked up so repeat scans are fast and work offline.
  • To keep the service running, diagnose problems, and protect it from abuse.

5. Third parties & sub-processors

The app calls a small number of outside services to do its job. Each is used only for the purpose named, and each has its own terms and privacy practices that apply to data it receives:

  • Open Food Facts — queried by barcode or search term to retrieve crowd-sourced product and nutrition data. A lookup sends the barcode or search text; it does not send your food log.
  • USDA FoodData Central — the U.S. Department of Agriculture’s nutrition database, queried as a fallback and second source for barcodes and whole-food searches. As above, a lookup sends only the query.
  • Anthropic (Claude API) — when you use “scan label,” the photograph of the nutrition-facts panel is sent to Anthropic’s Claude vision API, which reads it and returns the values as structured data. The image is sent only to perform this extraction for your request. Per Anthropic’s API terms, inputs submitted through the API are not used to train its models, and the app itself does not retain the image beyond the request — only the extracted nutrition fields are saved.
  • Wearable providers — Oura and Garmin — connected only at your explicit choice via OAuth. (Garmin’s integration is built, but usable only once Garmin’s partner-approval program grants API access.) See section 6 for exactly what is accessed and how it is protected.
  • Infrastructure / hosting — the service and its database run on general-purpose hosting infrastructure (for example a managed PostgreSQL provider such as Neon, and the server host chosen for deployment). These providers process and store data on our behalf to run the service.

Links to each provider’s own privacy policy: Open Food Facts, USDA FoodData Central, Anthropic, Oura, and Garmin.

6. Wearable & health data

This section describes the most sensitive data the app handles — the health and activity data from a connected wearable — and it applies specifically to Oura and Garmin (Garmin usable once Garmin’s partner-approval program grants API access).

  • Connected only with your consent. The app can read your wearable data only after you authorize it through the provider’s own OAuth consent screen. Nothing is accessed unless and until you click “Connect” and approve the requested scopes on the provider’s site.
  • What is accessed. With your authorization, the app reads your daily activity and energy-expenditure metrics (for example total daily calorie expenditure and steps) and closely related health metrics needed to compute an energy balance. It requests only the scopes required for this feature.
  • What it is used for. This data is used for exactly one purpose: to display your own energy balance (calories in versus calories out) and related stats to you, inside the app.
  • Never sold or shared. Your wearable and health data is never sold and never shared with third parties. It is not used for advertising, profiling, or any purpose other than showing you your stats.
  • Stored securely, server-side. The OAuth access and refresh tokens the provider issues are stored on the server and are never returned to or exposed in the browser/client. The client asks the server for a summary; the server holds the credentials.
  • You can revoke at any time. You may disconnect a wearable from within the app at any time, which deletes its stored tokens and stops any further access. You can also revoke the app’s access from your Oura or Garmin account settings. Disconnecting stops future access; to also erase previously stored data, see Retention & deletion or contact us.
  • Platform-terms compliance. The app’s access to and use of Oura data complies with Oura’s applicable developer/API terms, and its access to and use of Garmin data complies with Garmin’s applicable developer/API and platform terms. Wearable data is used consistently with those platforms’ requirements and with your authorization.

7. Data storage & security

All API keys and third-party credentials — including wearable OAuth tokens — are held server-side and are never sent to the browser. The server acts as a proxy so the client never sees a secret. Traffic to the deployed app is served over HTTPS.

An honest limitation: this is a personal project maintained by an individual, not a certified, audited, or enterprise-grade secure service. It carries no security certification (such as SOC 2 or ISO 27001) and makes no guarantee of absolute security. Depending on the deployment, stored tokens may not be encrypted at rest. You should weigh that before connecting sensitive accounts, and you should not treat the app as a system of record for health data. No method of transmission or storage is ever completely secure.

8. Data retention & deletion

Your food log, foods, and targets are kept until you delete them. You are in control:

  • Delete individual entries or foods from within the app.
  • Disconnect a wearable in the app to delete its stored tokens and stop further access.
  • Erase everything. Because there is no login, the most complete deletion is to remove the underlying data store — drop or clear the database (or delete the local data file) for a self-hosted deployment. For the operator-run instance, email us at the address below to request erasure of the data associated with a connected account, and we will delete it.

Some third parties (for example Anthropic, Oura, or Garmin) may retain logs of requests you make to them under their own retention policies; those are governed by their policies, not this one.

For anything you cannot do yourself on an operator-run instance, contact us at the address below and we will help you exercise these rights. You may withdraw wearable authorization at any time without affecting the rest of the app.
